What makes it work

Four 15-minute sessions of a linear 1–10 board game closed the number-line estimation gap for low-income preschoolers. The same game with colours instead of numbers, or on a circular board, did not.

  1. Straight, equal, numbered squares. Distance on the board matches number size.
  2. Say the number you land on. "6, 7", never "1, 2". Counting on is the active ingredient.
  3. Numbers always visible on every square.
  4. Small steps. A 1–2 spinner keeps every move short enough to count and check.
  5. Short, repeated sessions. 15–20 minutes, 4–6 times over 2–3 weeks.
  6. Tens structure for 1–100, with landmarks on 10, 20, 30…

No rewards, shops, power-ups or timers on counting: they pull attention off the numbers.

Sources: Laski & Siegler 2014 · WWC review · CMU summary
