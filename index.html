Items per session
Each item comes up in at least two levels. Fewer items means a shorter session and more repetition per item.

Privacy: the game runs in the browser. There are no accounts or tracking, and nothing is sent anywhere. Settings and your deck are saved on this device only.

  • Retrieval practice: students pull answers out of memory instead of re-reading. Recalling something strengthens memory more than reviewing it.
  • Feedback that explains: every mistake shows the right answer and a short reason or strategy, while the question is still fresh.
  • Spacing: a missed item comes back two questions later, and again in the final level. It never repeats straight away.
  • Interleaving: the final level mixes every item, so students have to work out which fact or strategy applies.
  • Scaffolding: new items arrive in small chunks. Choices go from 2 to 3 to 4, and time pressure only starts once items are familiar.
  • Mastery, low stakes: no lives and no game over. A level ends when every item has been answered correctly, and progress is never taken away.
  • Diagnostic distractors: wrong options in the built-in topics are common misconceptions (e.g. 1/4 → 0.4, "librería" = library), so the errors tell you something.
  • Formative data: the end screen shows first-try accuracy and which items to revisit. Copy report gives you a text summary you can paste anywhere.
